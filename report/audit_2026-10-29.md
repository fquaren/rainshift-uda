# Code and methodology audit, 2026-10-29

Five read-only audits ran in parallel over all ~11,500 lines of Python and shell: training core, evaluation and aggregation, AFM, shift tests (including P2 and the tests), and the launchers. I re-checked every item marked *verified* against the code, a config or a log. *Suspected* means the mechanism is plausible but its size has not been measured. Nothing has been fixed yet.

## A. Affects results already in PLAN.md

**A1. FDA sign-flips the target mean for about half the samples.** *Verified* (`uda.py:433-449`).
- What happens:
  - The inputs are z-scored, so a sample's per-channel spatial mean is negative about half the time. Its DC phase is then π.
  - The code swaps the DC *amplitude* for |target DC| and reattaches the *source* phase. The result has mean sign(source mean)·|target mean|.
  - Example: a europe sample with tcw mean −0.3, adapted towards melanesia (+4.4 in europe units), comes out at −4.4. That value lies outside both domains, and the sample is still trained against its original label.
  - Separately, at 80×80 and β = 0.01 the window `[cy-1:cy+1]` covers DC and only the −1 frequencies. Their Hermitian partners are not swapped, so `.real` half-applies the swap. The reference implementation uses `c-b:c+b+1`.
- Consequence:
  - All 18 seeded FDA runs measure this perturbation, not FDA.
  - That covers the "melanesia → europe FDA 51 % of budget", "FDA europe → melanesia 3/3 seeds", and FDA as a D2 candidate.
  - FDA results cannot be quoted until the code is fixed and FDA is retrained (18 runs).

**A2. DANN never aligns: the adversarial gradient on the encoder is about 5e-4 of the task gradient.** *Verified* (`train_unet.py:259-260`, `uda.py:280-310`, seed-42 europe→melanesia log).
- What happens:
  - One AdamW optimises task + 5e-4·BCE.
  - The discriminator's parameters see only the BCE gradient, which Adam rescales per parameter, so the discriminator learns at the full rate.
  - The encoder's reversed gradient is added to the task gradient *before* Adam's normalisation, so it is diluted.
- Evidence: the logged discriminator loss falls from 0.55 to 0.37 (chance is 0.693) and the run stops at epoch 9. The discriminator wins; nothing is aligned.
- Consequence:
  - PLAN already shows MMD is inert at λ = 0.1.
  - Claim 3's "two alignment losses fail the same way" therefore rests on two losses that never aligned anything.
  - DANN's source inflation is plausibly all BatchNorm mixing, as tgtfwd showed for MMD.
  - The GAP ablation's grid4 arm is the only alignment in the project that was demonstrably driven (gradient share 0.64–0.76).

**A3. λ̂ is one-sided.** *Verified* (`aggregate_seeds.py:190`).
- What happens:
  - λ̂ = E_T(oracle) − E_T(h_T). Ben-David's λ is the joint error ε_S + ε_T of one hypothesis.
  - `oracle_src` is computed but kept out of λ̂.
- Two-sided λ̂ as a share of the gap, recomputed by the audit from `oracle.csv`:

  | pair | two-sided | one-sided (current) |
  | --- | --- | --- |
  | melanesia→horn | 0.35 | 0.035 |
  | horn→melanesia | 0.21 | 0.18 |
  | europe→horn | 0.09 | 0.04 |
  | melanesia→europe | 0.05 | 0.02 |
  | horn→europe | 0.02 | 0.004 |

- At the 0.1 mm/h floor the source side has never been scored: `field_mean_check.py` scores only the target split. So the P14 λ̂ is one-sided by construction.
- Consequence: claim 2's λ̂ must be defined explicitly and both sides reported. Either the "λ̂ ≈ 0" sentence changes, or it is stated as the target-side excess.

**A4. The binned conditional-shift test (claim 1, P1) is biased towards finding shift.** *Verified* (`conditional_shift_binned.py:110-155`).
- The null splits one domain, so within every coarse (CAPE, TCW) quantile bin both halves have the same covariate distribution.
  - Across domains, A's pixels can sit at one end of a wide bin and B's at the other.
  - Under pure covariate shift that within-bin mismatch alone gives W1 > 0, and the null contains none of it.
  - The docstring's claim that the null "absorbs within-bin covariate variation" is false for exactly this term.
- The statistic is in log(P + 1e-5) of 5×5 block means.
  - A dry pixel sits at −11.5 and a wet one near −2 to 0, so W1 ≈ 9.5 × |Δ dry fraction| plus the wet part.
  - This is the same floor artefact as P3/P14.
- `min_count = 200` pixels is 1/8 of one 40×40 frame, so a single storm can make a bin "supported".
- The verdict compares with the null mean using fixed cutoffs. It ignores the null spread and the sampling uncertainty of W1_cond.
  - The null halves have 300 frames against 600 in the cross comparison, which makes the null conservative.
  - Under that comparison horn ↔ melanesia (0.395) is above the week p95 of both domains, so "inconclusive" may understate it.
- Consequence: today's P1 numbers (6.2×, 1.9×) are not usable as they stand. Fixes:
  - a conditional-permutation null (labels permuted within fine sub-bins), which keeps the coarse-bin mismatch;
  - the D1 floors;
  - wet fraction and wet amount reported separately;
  - min_count in distinct weeks;
  - equal frame counts;
  - an empirical p-value.

**A5. NGG's W1 denominator z-scores each domain by its own statistics.** *Verified* (`covariate_shift_analysis/compute_shift.py:161, 321`).
- This is the CLAUDE.md normalisation trap applied to the shift measure.
- The audit's rough recomputation reverses the pair ranking and inflates NGG 2–5×.
- The W1 cube cannot be regenerated: it reads `.npy` and `stats.json`, which no longer exist.
- Consequence: drop the NGG appendix (P13), or rebuild it from Zarr in source-normalised space.

**A6. PLAN.md says phase-2 patience is −1. Every phase-2 config says 5, and the oracle says 8.** *Verified* (configs).
- The "known issue" never happened.
- The real asymmetry: methods stop at epochs 7–14 under patience 5, on a 25-epoch cosine schedule. The oracle gets patience 8 and joint selection.
- That favours the oracle, so it biases λ̂ downwards.

**A7. Source and target batches are time-locked.** *Verified* by reading (`dataset_zarr.py:316-334`); the shared calendar is not checked.
- All domains have 175,320 steps, identical train chunk ids and the same RNG seed. So source batch k and target batch k hold the same timestamps, every step of every epoch.
- Minibatch MMD/DANN/JDOT terms compare season- and hour-matched pairs, which biases batch discrepancies below the marginal ones. FDA always borrows the same timestamp's amplitude.
- Also *verified*: the order is identical in every epoch, worse than "same across seeds"; the dataset comment claims otherwise.

**A8. JDOT and its diagnostic.**
- *Suspected:* JDOT's Sinkhorn at reg = 0.001 with 50 iterations is far from converged. The row marginals are not enforced, which pulls target predictions towards the commonest (dry) source labels.
- *Verified:* the "feature term = 0.3 % of transport cost" (`diagnose_jdot.py`) comes from one batch of the first two chunks, in eval-mode BatchNorm.

**A9. The GAP ablation was read only in `mse_std`** (the 1e-5 floor), and its `results.csv` files have no `mse_log_*` columns. *Verified.* Its grid4 conclusion cannot be re-read at D1 without re-scoring.

## B. Affects jobs queued now

**B1. P16 few-shot (smoke 66685374, arrays 66685375/77/79): BatchNorm running statistics are re-estimated on the N target samples.** *Verified* (`finetune_unet.py:90`).
- `model.train()` for 500 steps at momentum 0.1 replaces the source statistics with those of the subset. At N = 1 chunk that subset is one 200-hour window.
- The small-N end of the curve is therefore "AdaBN on one window, plus labels". AdaBN alone hurts in 5 of 6 pairs.
- There is also no gradient clipping, unlike training.
- Fix before it runs: keep BatchNorm in eval mode (frozen statistics) during fine-tuning, or report both variants.

**B2. AFM phase 1 (66685381): nothing invalidating.**
- The flow-matching path, time direction, sampler, EMA use, selection and ensembles are correct.
- Design question, not a bug: the flow loss trains μ through z = μ + σε with no detach (`afm.py:129-136`). μ is then not a pure MSE regressor, which weakens the "differs only in the objective" comparison of μ with the UNet. Check against Fotiadis et al.
- Cost only: a target batch is streamed every step even for `none`.

**B3. P19 seeds 43/44, their scoring, and UNet acceptance: clean.** The scoring jobs carry SEED, `TARGET=melanesia` and the right `TREE_SUFFIX`. *Verified* from the submission commands.

## C. Latent tool bugs to fix before their next use

- **`aggregate_seeds.py:79-88` scales every metric by s_S or s_S².** That includes `mse_log_e01`/`crps_log_e01`, which are already in log-mm units. The first D1 aggregation (P16, AFM, re-tables) would repeat the mixed-units bug. *Verified.*
- **`evaluate.py batch` and `eval_array.sh` score any `best.pt`, including runs still training, and an existing `metrics.json` then makes the score stick.** `afm_seed42/` will hold phase 1 and phase 2 side by side. Require `config.json` (written at the end) before scoring. *Verified.*
- **Rank histograms and spread are computed in standardised log(P + 1e-5) space.**
  - Dry truth sits at the floor constant, so ranks on the 74–94 % dry pixels measure sub-floor scatter.
  - Spread uses ddof = 0 with a pixel-mean of std and no (M+1)/M factor.
  - No ensemble-mean error is saved.
  - All of this biases 3.4 towards "over-confident". Rank and score wet pixels, or ranks after flooring at 0.1 mm.
- **The AFM UDA arm is not the UNet treatment.**
  - The flow net, which makes the ensemble, is never aligned.
  - The DANN discriminator runs at 0.1×LR with wd 1e-4.
  - λ is relative to `flow + 0.1·enc`.
  - There is no `--mmd_pool`/`--mmd_unbiased`.
  - D2 has to account for this, or the AFM "UDA fails" result is partly by construction.
- **AFM AdaBN re-estimates flow-net BatchNorm on x_t = (1−t)z + t·μ.** Training uses y and sampling uses samples.
- **Paths that would normalise the target with its own stats or cannot run:**
  - `evaluate.py single` and `plotting.py` normalise the target with target stats, read NPY, and plot `best_adabn.pt` for the source.
  - The `sinkhorn.py` OT transform cannot run on the Zarr data.
- **Overwrite and safety:**
  - `compute_stats.sh` writes `normalization_stats.json` into `raw_data/rainshift` (rule 3) when `FORCE=1`.
  - `evaluate.sh` rewrites `ngg_v2/` on every run.
  - `run_analysis.sh` overwrites the W1 cube.
  - `copy.sh` nests copies inside existing directories.
- **`run_exp_unet.sh` mis-runs in several ways:**
  - Array indices shift if any `base_hp` file is missing.
  - `PATIENCE` defaults to −1 for hand-submitted phase 2/oracle.
  - `launch_sbatch.sh` passes `PATIENCE` where phase 1 reads `PATIENCE_P1`.
- **Stale scripts on the old `unet/` tree or the GH200 container:** `run_diagnose_jdot.sh`, `run_plotting.sh`, `data/run_conversion.sh`, `compute_stats.sh`.
- **Weak tests:** `test_evaluate_metrics.py` and `test_conditional_shift.py` test consistency with the same formula, not correctness against raw units.

## D. P2 (`conditional_shift.py`, not run): what must change first

1. **One draw of half models gives no training variance.** N_A and N_B come from a single pair of half models, and the chunk bootstrap holds the models fixed. P1's week null varies ±45 % between draws, so R_half could sit at 0.6 or 1.6 under a shared conditional with a CI that excludes 1. Replicate: 3 seeds × 2 halves per domain, 18 runs, and report the spread over model pairs. The test's i.i.d. pixel noise cannot catch this; simulate smooth per-model error fields and check coverage.
2. **The independence the null needs is not there.** All half models share seed 42, and D includes same-year pairs (A/h0, B/h0) while every N pair uses disjoint years. Use different seeds per half, and form D only from cross-year pairs. Same cost.
3. **Overlap:**
   - report R at P(A|x) ≥ 0.1 / 0.3 / 0.5; if R falls towards 1 as the overlap tightens, the excess is extrapolation;
   - the statics are never inside the overlap, so a masked-statics half-model arm is the only way to remove them;
   - enforce class balance.
4. **Bootstrap blocks of ~30 days, not 200 h.**
5. **Crash safety:** write each domain's npz as it finishes, and add an analysis-only `--from_npz` mode.
6. **Split hygiene:**
   - drop chunks adjacent to block boundaries (4 places where halves touch);
   - raise if the half filter empties the list.
7. **Power:**
   - nothing shows the test can detect a known conditional difference; add a positive control (one domain against a copy with a known target transformation);
   - b2_half estimates the half-data bias difference, not the full-model one.

Even with all of this the test stays asymmetric: R ≈ 1 is evidence of a shared conditional, while R ≫ 1 is not proof of shift.
